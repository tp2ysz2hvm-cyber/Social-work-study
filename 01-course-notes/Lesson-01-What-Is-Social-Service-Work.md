# Lesson 1 — What Is Social Service Work?

## Learning goals
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:
- Explain what a Social Service Worker does.
- Describe the difference between helping and rescuing.
- Identify the importance of boundaries, ethics, and client self-determination.
- Recognize the basic helping process.

## 1. What is Social Service Work?
Social Service Work is about helping people cope with problems, access resources, build strengths, and make changes in their lives.

A Social Service Worker may support people dealing with:
- poverty or housing problems
- mental health challenges
- addiction
- family conflict
- disability
- unemployment
- abuse or violence
- isolation
- grief or crisis

The worker does not simply tell people what to do. The goal is to work **with** the client, not take control of the client.

## 2. Helping vs. rescuing
Helping means supporting a person while respecting their ability to make choices.

Rescuing means taking over, making decisions for the person, or creating dependence.

### Example
A client says they are behind on rent.

**Rescuing response:**
“I’ll call everyone for you and fix this.”

**Helping response:**
“Let’s look at the options together. What have you already tried, and where would you like to start?”

The second response keeps the client involved in the solution.

## 3. Client self-determination
Self-determination means clients have the right to make their own choices whenever they are legally and safely able to do so.

A worker can:
- give information
- explain options
- discuss consequences
- offer support

A worker should not pressure a client into choosing what the worker personally thinks is best.

## 4. Professional boundaries
A professional relationship is different from a friendship.

Boundaries help protect both the client and the worker.

Examples of healthy boundaries:
- keeping personal information limited and purposeful
- avoiding romantic or sexual relationships with clients
- not lending or borrowing money
- not making the client dependent on you
- following agency rules
- keeping communication professional

Being caring does not mean becoming personally responsible for the client’s life.

## 5. Ethics
Ethics are principles that guide professional behaviour.

Important ideas include:
- respect
- dignity
- confidentiality
- honesty
- fairness
- competence
- professional responsibility
- avoiding harm

Sometimes ethical principles can conflict. For example, confidentiality is important, but there may be situations where safety or law requires information to be shared.

## 6. Strengths-based practice
A strengths-based approach asks:

**“What is working, what abilities does this person already have, and what resources can we build on?”**

Instead of seeing only problems, the worker notices strengths such as:
- persistence
- family support
- employment skills
- past successes
- cultural/community connections
- coping skills
- willingness to seek help

## 7. The basic helping process
A simple version of the helping process is:

1. **Engagement** — build trust and rapport.
2. **Assessment** — understand the situation, needs, risks, strengths, and goals.
3. **Planning** — agree on realistic goals and possible actions.
4. **Intervention** — carry out the plan, provide support, referrals, advocacy, or skill-building.
5. **Evaluation** — check whether the plan is helping.
6. **Termination/transition** — end or transfer services appropriately.

## 8. A practical example
A client says:

> “I lost my job, I’m behind on rent, and I don’t know what to do.”

A Social Service Worker might:
- listen and clarify the immediate problem
- check for urgent risks such as eviction, lack of food, or safety concerns
- identify what the client has already tried
- discuss income-support or housing resources
- help the client prioritize next steps
- make referrals where appropriate
- document the interaction
- follow up on progress

The worker does not promise to solve everything. The worker helps the client move from crisis toward a workable plan.

## Key terms
- **Self-determination:** the client’s right to make their own choices.
- **Boundaries:** limits that keep the helping relationship professional and safe.
- **Strengths-based practice:** focusing on abilities, resources, and resilience as well as problems.
- **Assessment:** gathering and organizing information to understand the client’s situation.
- **Intervention:** actions taken to support agreed-upon goals.
- **Confidentiality:** protecting private client information, subject to legal and safety limits.

## One-sentence takeaway
A Social Service Worker helps people identify needs, strengths, options, and resources while respecting their dignity, choices, and independence.
