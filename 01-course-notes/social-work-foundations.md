# Social Work Foundations

## Person-in-environment
People are understood in the context of their relationships, communities, culture, institutions, resources, and social conditions — not as isolated problems.

## Strengths perspective
Look for abilities, resources, resilience, knowledge, relationships, and past successes alongside needs and challenges.

## Client self-determination
Support clients in making informed choices about their own lives within legal, ethical, and safety limits.

## Empathy
Try to understand the client's experience and communicate that understanding without assuming your experience is the same as theirs.

## Professional boundaries
Maintain a relationship focused on the client's needs and the worker's professional role.

## Confidentiality
Protect private client information and understand the specific legal, ethical, and agency limits to confidentiality.

## Anti-oppressive practice
Pay attention to power, discrimination, barriers, privilege, and social structures that can affect clients and access to services.

## Cultural humility
Remain curious and reflective rather than assuming expertise about another person's culture or identity.

## Advocacy
Support people in accessing rights, resources, services, and fair treatment while respecting their goals and choices.

## Reflection
Regularly examine your reactions, assumptions, decisions, strengths, mistakes, and use of power in practice.
